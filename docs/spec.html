Yard Ledger

Rental management for a construction equipment yard — jacks, spans, sheets, cup-lock. An append-only movement ledger with day-wise rent accrual, offline-capable admin surface, and a token-linked customer portal.

Stack Next.js 15 · Neon · Drizzle · Better Auth
Client PWA, offline-first
Native app Not in scope
Region India · INR · Asia/Kolkata
00

Brief for the coding agent

This document is the single source of truth. Build strictly to it. Where it is silent, choose the simplest option that does not contradict anything written here, and record the choice in /docs/decisions.md.

Non-negotiable rules

  1. The movements table is append-only. Never UPDATE or DELETE a movement row. Corrections are reversing entries. This single rule is what makes offline sync, auditing, and dispute resolution work.
  2. Never store a computed balance or stock count as a mutable column. Availability and dues are always derived by replaying events. Cache in a materialised view if needed, never in a hand-maintained integer.
  3. All money is bigint paise. No floats anywhere, including in JSON payloads. Format to rupees only at the render layer.
  4. All dates for rent purposes are date, not timestamp. Rent is counted in whole days in Asia/Kolkata. Timestamps are for audit only.
  5. Write the accrual engine first (§03), as a pure function with no I/O, and make every test vector in §03.5 pass before building a single screen.
  6. Every table carries org_id. The product ships single-tenant but must not require a migration to become multi-tenant.
Build order

Work milestone by milestone through §13. Do not start a milestone until the previous one's acceptance criteria pass. Stop and ask before deviating from the schema in §04 or the sync protocol in §07.

01

Scope

What the business does

A yard owns a fixed quantity of each equipment type. Contractors take equipment to a site, keep it for weeks or months, return it in parts, and pay periodically. Rent accrues per item per day held. Items get lost or damaged. A customer's relationship with the yard is a running account, not a series of discrete orders.

In scope — v1

Explicitly out of scope

  • Flutter or any native app. The PWA is the mobile app; it installs to the home screen and works offline.
  • Online payment collection. Payments are recorded, not processed. No gateway in v1.
  • SMS or OTP login. Customer access is by signed link (§05.4).
  • GST filing, e-way bills, accounting exports. Bills are proforma. Revisit after v1.
  • Transport / vehicle dispatch management.
Design constraint

The primary admin user is standing in a dusty yard, on a mid-range Android phone, one-handed, possibly with no signal. Every admin screen must be usable at 360 px wide with touch targets of at least 44 px, and must never block on a network call.

02

Domain model

The central idea

Do not model this as orders with statuses. Model it as a ledger of movement events. Everything the business asks for is then a derivation rather than a feature:

Business questionDerivation
How many jacks are available?owned − Σ(ISSUE.qty) + Σ(RETURN.qty) across all open accounts
What does this customer owe?Replay their movements through the accrual engine, subtract payments
Add items to an existing unreturned orderAnother ISSUE on the same account. No special case.
Partial returnRETURN with qty less than outstanding. No special case.
Is the order closed?Outstanding qty is zero for every item on the account
What changed and who did it?The ledger is the audit log. Nothing else needed.

Entities

Customer  identified by mobile number. May hold several accounts.
   └─ Account  one running khata, usually per construction site.
       ├─ Movement  ISSUE or RETURN of one item, one quantity, one date. Append-only.
       ├─ Payment   money received against the account.
       ├─ Adjustment discount, transport charge, round-off, waiver.
       └─ Bill      frozen snapshot of dues for a period. Immutable once issued.

Item  equipment type owned by the yard. Referenced by movements.
User  a staff login. Distinct from Customer — customers do not log in.

Invariants

  • An account's outstanding qty for any item must never go below zero. The server rejects a RETURN that would breach this.
  • An account cannot be closed while any item has outstanding qty above zero.
  • rate_snapshot is copied onto every ISSUE row at creation. Changing an item's master rate must never alter historical rent.
  • A bill, once issued, is immutable. Corrections are a credit adjustment plus a new bill.
  • A movement's moved_at date may be back-dated but never set in the future.

Movement types

TypeSignMeaning
ISSUE+qtyEquipment leaves the yard. Starts accrual.
RETURN−qtyEquipment comes back in usable condition. Stops accrual.
RETURN_DAMAGED−qtyComes back unusable. Stops accrual, charges repair or replacement.
LOST−qtyNever coming back. Stops accrual, charges replacement rate, reduces effective owned stock.
REVERSAL∓qtyCancels an earlier movement. Carries reverses_id. Admin+ only.
03

Rent accrual engine

This is the heart of the product and the source of every customer dispute. Build it as a pure, dependency-free module at /lib/accrual/ with exhaustive unit tests. It must never import a database client.

03.1 Billing configuration

Stored per org in settings, passed into the engine as an argument — never read from a global.

KeyDefaultEffect
day_count_modeinclusive_startinclusive_start: issue day billed, return day not. inclusive_both: both billed. Yard convention — confirm with owner before launch.
minimum_days15Every issued unit is billed for at least this many days even if returned sooner.
minimum_days_appliesper_issue_lotMinimum is evaluated per ISSUE lot, not per account.
roundingnearest_rupeeApplied once to the final line total, never per day.
damage_charge_modereplacement_rateAlternative: manual — admin types the amount per movement.
accrual_stops_on_billfalseRent keeps accruing after billing until items physically return.

03.2 Lot-based algorithm

Naive "outstanding × days" fails the moment a minimum-days rule exists, because you cannot tell which units are being returned. Use FIFO lot consumption: each ISSUE creates a lot; each RETURN consumes from the oldest open lot first.

// Pure. No I/O. All dates are plain YYYY-MM-DD strings in Asia/Kolkata.

type Movement = {
  id: string; itemId: string; type: MovementType;
  qty: number; movedAt: string; rateSnapshot: number; // paise/unit/day
  replacementSnapshot: number; manualCharge?: number;
}

type AccrualResult = {
  lines: LineItem[];        // one per lot, for the bill
  rentTotal: number;        // paise
  damageTotal: number;      // paise
  outstanding: Record<itemId, number>;
  openLots: Lot[];          // still out, for the ageing report
}

function accrue(movements, config, asOf) {
  // 1. Drop movements cancelled by a REVERSAL.
  // 2. Sort by (movedAt asc, createdAt asc). Stable — same input, same output.
  // 3. Group by itemId. Lots never cross item boundaries.
  // 4. Per item, walk the sorted movements:
  //      ISSUE  -> push lot { qty, from: movedAt, rate, remaining: qty }
  //      RETURN -> consume qty from oldest open lots, FIFO.
  //                For each consumed slice, close it:
  //                  days = dayCount(lot.from, movedAt, config)
  //                  days = max(days, config.minimum_days)
  //                  rent += slice * days * lot.rate
  //      RETURN_DAMAGED -> same as RETURN for rent,
  //                plus damage += slice * (manualCharge ?? replacementSnapshot)
  //      LOST   -> same as RETURN_DAMAGED, and flag lot as lost
  // 5. Any lot still open at the end accrues up to `asOf`:
  //      days = max(dayCount(lot.from, asOf, config), config.minimum_days)
  // 6. Round each line total per config. Sum.
}

function dayCount(from, to, config) {
  const raw = differenceInCalendarDays(to, from);
  return config.day_count_mode === 'inclusive_both' ? raw + 1 : Math.max(raw, 1);
}
Do not skip

FIFO lot consumption is mandatory. Without it, a customer who takes 100 jacks in January and 100 more in June, then returns 100, gets billed incorrectly — and the error compounds silently for months. There is no shortcut here.

03.3 Rate changes mid-rental

The rate that applies is the one frozen on the ISSUE lot. Editing items.rate_per_day affects only future issues. If the yard genuinely renegotiates an ongoing rental, the admin records a REVERSAL of the original issue and a fresh ISSUE at the new rate on the changeover date. Do not build a rate-history table in v1.

03.4 Account balance

balance = rentTotal + damageTotal + Σ(adjustments where type=charge)
          − Σ(payments) − Σ(adjustments where type=credit)

status = balance > 0  ? 'due'
       : balance < 0  ? 'advance'
       :                'settled'

// Payment status shown to customer, per bill:
//   paid       — allocated >= grand_total
//   partial    — 0 < allocated < grand_total
//   pending    — allocated == 0, within due date
//   overdue    — allocated < grand_total, past due date

Payments are allocated to bills oldest-first automatically. Store the allocation in payment_allocations so a partially-paid bill can be shown accurately.

Derived views

create view v_item_stock as
select i.id, i.org_id, i.name, i.qty_owned,
  coalesce(sum(case m.type
    when 'ISSUE' then m.qty
    when 'RETURN' then -m.qty
    when 'RETURN_DAMAGED' then -m.qty
    when 'LOST' then -m.qty
    else 0 end), 0) as qty_out,
  i.qty_owned - coalesce(sum(...), 0) as qty_available
from items i
left join movements m on m.item_id = i.id
  and not exists (select 1 from movements r where r.reverses_id = m.id)
group by i.id;

Account balances are not a view — they require the FIFO engine, so compute them in application code and cache per account in Redis-free memory with a short TTL, or recompute on read. At this data volume, recomputing is fine.

05.4 Customer portal access

Customers do not have passwords. Access is by an unguessable signed link that the admin shares over WhatsApp.

  1. Admin taps Share statement on a customer.
  2. Server generates 32 random bytes, stores the SHA-256 hash in portal_tokens with a 90-day expiry, returns the raw token once.
  3. Client opens https://wa.me/<mobile>?text=<encoded message with URL>.
  4. Customer opens /s/<token> — read-only view of their accounts, outstanding items, bills, payments, and current dues. Landing sets a scoped cookie so refreshes work.
  5. Admin can revoke any token from the customer page.

Rate-limit /s/[token] to 20 requests/minute per IP. Log last_used_at. If the same customer needs several links, reuse the active token rather than minting new ones.

Why not OTP

Phone OTP costs money per message and needs a DLT-registered sender in India. The signed link costs nothing, works on any phone, and matches how these customers already communicate. Keep the portal_tokens table so that adding OTP later is an additive change, not a rewrite.

07

Offline-first architecture

The append-only ledger is what makes this tractable. Two devices offline never write the same cell, because nobody writes stock = 42 — they write issued 10. Merging is a union of rows.

07.1 Client storage

LayerTechContents
App shellService worker (Workbox)Precached routes, JS, CSS, fonts. Cache-first.
Read mirrorDexie / IndexedDBitems, customers, open accounts, movements for open accounts, settings
Write queueDexie outbox tablePending mutations with client-generated UUIDs
Binary queueDexie blob storePhotos and signatures awaiting upload

07.2 Write path

1. User submits an issue/return/payment form.
2. Generate client_uuid = crypto.randomUUID() per line.
3. Validate locally with the shared Zod schema + local outstanding check.
4. Write to Dexie: local tables updated optimistically, row flagged `pending`.
5. Append to `outbox`. UI returns immediately — never await the network.
6. Background sync (or a 30s timer, or an `online` event) drains the outbox.
7. Server upserts with ON CONFLICT (org_id, client_uuid) DO NOTHING.
   Retries are therefore free and idempotent.
8. On 2xx: mark rows `synced`, clear the pending badge.
   On 4xx business rejection: move to `needs_attention`, surface in the UI.
   On 5xx / network error: leave queued, exponential backoff, retry forever.

07.3 Read path

Pull by cursor: GET /api/sync/pull?cursor=<last server_seq> returns rows with a higher sequence, capped at 500 per page. Use a monotonic server_seq bigint column (a sequence, not a timestamp — clocks are unreliable) on every syncable table.

07.4 Conflicts and what to do about them

SituationResolution
Two devices issue the same item offline; total exceeds owned stockAccept both. The equipment physically left the yard; the ledger must reflect reality. Raise a "negative availability" alert on the stock screen for an admin to reconcile.
Return recorded offline exceeds outstanding at merge timeReject that line only. Other lines in the batch still commit. Write to sync_rejections, show it in the device's "Needs attention" queue with the reason and the option to correct the qty and resubmit.
Same movement submitted twice (retry, double-tap)Silently absorbed by the client_uuid unique constraint.
Customer created offline on two devices with the same mobileServer merges on (org_id, mobile), returns the canonical id, client rewrites its local foreign keys.
Device clock is wrongServer records its own created_at. moved_at is user-entered and defaults to a date the user confirms on screen, so a bad clock is visible and correctable.

07.5 Honest offline UI

  • Persistent status chip in the header: Online · Offline — 4 pending · Syncing… · 2 need attention.
  • Stock availability while offline shows the sync age: "Available 214 · as of 2h ago". Never present a stale number as authoritative.
  • Money screens (bills, balances) are read-only offline and clearly stamped with the sync time. Payments can still be recorded offline.
  • Rows not yet synced carry a small pending marker. Do not hide them.
Scope discipline

Offline is required only for: record issue, record return, create customer, open account, record payment, and view open accounts. Everything else — reports, bill generation, settings, user management — may require a connection and should say so plainly rather than failing strangely.

/s/[token]              Customer portal (public, read-only)

08.2 Account screen — the one that matters

Everything an admin needs while standing in front of a contractor, on one scrollable page:

  • Header — customer name, mobile (tap to call, tap to WhatsApp), site name, days open, status.
  • Balance card — rent accrued to date, damages, charges, paid, net due in large type. Hidden for the staff role.
  • Currently out — item, qty out, oldest issue date, days held, accruing per day. Tap a row to return that item.
  • Actions — Issue more · Record return · Record payment · Generate bill · Share statement.
  • Ledger — reverse-chronological movements, payments, bills, and adjustments interleaved. Each row shows who entered it and when. Long-press a movement to reverse it (admin+).

08.3 Issue flow

Customer  →  search or create inline (name + mobile is enough)
Account   →  pick open account or create with a site name
Date      →  defaults to today, editable, never future
Items     →  searchable list with live availability; qty stepper per row
             running total of rent/day shown as items are added
Proof     →  optional photo of the loaded vehicle, optional signature pad
Confirm   →  summary screen, then one commit
Result    →  gate pass number, Share on WhatsApp, Print

The whole flow must complete in under 20 seconds for a repeat customer, and must work with the network off.

08.4 Customer portal /s/[token]

Single page, no navigation, mobile-only design. Shows: outstanding items with days held, total due in large type, list of bills with paid/pending status, payment history, and the yard's phone number. No prices per day unless settings.show_rates_to_customer is on. No login, no editing, no ordering.

08.5 Visual direction

Light theme throughout. Dense, high-contrast, legible in bright sun. System font stack for speed. Numbers in a tabular monospace so columns align. Colour used only for state — green settled, amber pending, red overdue, grey synced — never for decoration. No animation beyond 150 ms state transitions. Bottom tab bar on mobile: Home · Issue · Return · Accounts · More.

Billing & delivery

Bill generation

  1. Admin picks an account and a period (defaults: last bill's period_to + 1 day → today).
  2. Server runs the accrual engine over that period, produces line items per lot.
  3. Preview screen shows every line with an editable adjustments row.
  4. On confirm, the bill is written with lines frozen as JSONB and an invoice number from settings.next_invoice_no (allocated inside the transaction).
  5. The bill is now immutable. Corrections require a credit adjustment and a new bill.

PDF

Server-side render at /api/bills/[id]/pdf using @react-pdf/renderer. A4 and 80 mm thermal variants. Deterministic — the same bill id always produces byte-identical output. No headless browser (too heavy for the free tier).

WhatsApp delivery

No WhatsApp Business API in v1 — it costs money and needs approval. Instead, build a message composer that opens wa.me/<mobile>?text=<encoded> with a short summary plus the signed portal link. The admin taps once and sends from their own WhatsApp.

Sample message:

Amount due: ₹5,700
Full statement: https://app.example.com/s/xxxxxxxx

Overdue reminders

A daily Vercel Cron at 09:00 IST builds a "to remind" list of accounts overdue past due_on. It does not send anything automatically — it surfaces a queue on the dashboard where the admin taps through, one WhatsApp at a time. Zero cost, and a human stays in the loop with contractors they know personally.

Cost ceiling

Realistically free up to roughly 50 active accounts and a few thousand movements a month. The first thing to exceed a free tier is R2 storage if every gate pass gets a photo — compress client-side to ≤ 200 KB WebP before upload, which buys years of headroom. Neon's 0.5 GB is far beyond what this data volume needs. Set a calendar reminder to check Neon storage quarterly.

Backups

A GitHub Actions workflow runs pg_dump nightly and commits the gzipped dump to a private repo, keeping 30 days. Free, and it means the yard's ledger survives an account problem. Test the restore once before go-live — an untested backup is not a backup.

Conventions & guardrails

Structure

/app                routes and route handlers only — thin
/lib/accrual        pure engine, no I/O          ← test heavily
/lib/db             drizzle schema, client, queries
/lib/auth           better-auth config, capabilities, guard
/lib/sync           outbox, dexie schema, drain, cursor
/lib/validation     zod schemas shared client + server
/components/ui      primitives
/components/domain  IssueForm, ReturnSheet, LedgerRow, BalanceCard
/docs/decisions.md  every judgement call made while building

Rules

  • Route handlers contain no business logic. They authenticate, validate, call a function in /lib, and serialise.
  • Every mutation runs in a transaction. Movement batches commit atomically per gate pass, except that a business rejection of one line in an offline sync push rejects only that line.
  • Money never touches a float. Parse rupee input to paise at the edge; format paise to rupees at the render layer. One utility each way, used everywhere.
  • Dates for accrual are YYYY-MM-DD strings throughout. Never pass a JS Date into the accrual engine.
  • Zod schemas are defined once and imported by both the client form and the server handler. Same rules offline and online.
  • Error codes are constants in a shared enum, not string literals scattered around.
  • No any. No disabled TypeScript errors. Strict mode from commit one.
  • Loading and empty states are designed, not afterthoughts. An empty account screen invites the first issue; it does not say "No data".

Interface copy

Plain, active, specific, sentence case. The button says Record return and the toast says Return recorded. Errors explain what happened and what to do: "Only 6 jacks are out on this account — check the quantity." Never "Something went wrong."

Stop and ask

Confirm with the yard owner before finalising: the day-counting convention, the minimum rental period, whether the issue day and return day are both billed, how damage is priced, and whether customers may see per-day rates. These five answers are worth more than any amount of code, and getting them wrong invalidates every bill the system produces.
